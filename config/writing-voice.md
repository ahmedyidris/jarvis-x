# Ahmed's writing voice

**Written by Ahmed, 2026-09-15.** The placeholder marker that used to sit on
the first line is gone, so `code/content-draft.js` will now draft.

(That marker is deliberately not written out here. `loadVoiceProfile()` looks
for the literal string anywhere in the file, so naming it in prose puts the
profile straight back into the unfilled state — which is exactly what happened
on the first attempt at this file. Same shape as the kill-switch detector
flagging its own write-up: the check has no way to tell a mention from a
claim, and the fix belongs in the prose rather than in a cleverer check.)

**The sample below is VERBATIM and must stay that way.** Not tidied, not
spell-checked, not de-run-on'd. Its whole value is that it is exactly how he
writes when he is not performing — the typos and the long middle sentence are
signal, not noise. Anyone "cleaning it up" removes the only real data here.

## What it should NOT sound like

- **No fancy words.** His own rule: "I rarely use fancy words and I try to keep
  it basic and simple." If a shorter, plainer word exists, it is the right one.
- **No royal we.** See the rules below — this one is worth repeating here
  because "we" is the single most likely way a draft stops sounding like him.

**This section is thinner than the others and he knows it.** It is the half
most people skip, and it is the most useful half. Worth adding to over time —
concretely, with things that made him wince in an actual draft, rather than
abstractions. Until then the two above are real and the rest is not invented.

## Recurring rules

- **"I", never "we".** In his words: *"I say I no we's, its me myself and I
  when I speak, ther's no royal we."*
- **English first.** His reasoning: it is the language the code is in, and the
  likelier route to income from international content or trading. Arabic is a
  later phase (PLAN_5 §6.2's "English first then Arabic"), not a parallel one.
- **Basic and simple over clever.** Short words, plain sentences.

## The thing he keeps coming back to

> "keep trying and or die trying ... if it's a dead end then stop and
> reevaluate."

Worth holding both halves. It is not stubbornness for its own sake — the
second clause is the part that makes the first one sane, and a draft that
renders him as relentless-without-judgement gets him wrong.

## What he is actually building toward

Stated plainly, because it should shape what gets written rather than sit in a
planning document nobody re-reads: **$1000 a month, so he has more time with
his family.** Not scale, not an exit. A draft that reaches for hustle-culture
ambition is aiming at a target he explicitly did not set.
